# Show an exact (Clopper-Pearson) interval next to the Wilson one in the Lab
kind: better test
idea: Add an exact binomial interval (the Clopper-Pearson interval, found by bisection on exact binomial tails) beside the Wilson interval in the probability package, and have the Lab show both for every sampled number, with the exact coverage of each at that sample size. The Lab then says "at least 95 % of the time" instead of "95 %".
why it's interesting: Manager W's audit measured that the 95 % Wilson label is off by 5 points at moderate probabilities. I re-measured on a finer grid and for rare events it is off by about 10 points (83.8 % coverage at 10 draws), exactly the regime of rare transitions in the Lost Lands data and of rare evidence in Wumpus-style questions. Sam's reporting rule asks for claims that can be cross-checked, and a coverage label that is wrong is the opposite of that.
smallest experiment: A function exact_interval(successes, trials, confidence) using Fraction arithmetic for the tails and bisection on a Fraction grid; a test that its exact coverage over p in steps of 0.0005 is at least the nominal level for every n up to 60, and the same test shows Wilson failing it for the n values above. Then show both intervals in the next sampled-number instrument.
reuses: dsdk.prob wilson_interval, standard_error, Estimate; the exact-fraction convention of dsdk.prob.exact; the coverage-by-seeds tests already in tests/prob.
probe: Read-only, binomial sums on the real wilson_interval. Exact coverage of the 95 % Wilson interval, minimum over true p in steps of 0.0005 (against the 0.01-step grid in brackets): 83.8 % at n=10 near p=0.0175 (90.4 %), 84.8 % at n=30 near p=0.0055 (93.0 % at p=0.3), 86.1 % at n=100 near p=0.0015 (92.1 %). A Clopper-Pearson interval found by bisection (float, 60 steps) has minimum coverage 96.1 % at n=10 and 95.4 % at n=30 on the 0.005-step grid, and is wider: at 5 of 10 draws it is (0.187, 0.813) against Wilson's (0.237, 0.763). Command: sum of comb(n,k) p^k (1-p)^(n-k) over the k whose interval contains p.
score: surprise=3 cost=2 reuse=4
