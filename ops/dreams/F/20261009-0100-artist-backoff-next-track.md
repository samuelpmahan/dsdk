# Back off from track to artist when the next-track model has never seen the track
kind: hypothesis
idea: Predict the next track with P(next | track) when that exact track was seen on other days, and otherwise back off to P(next artist | this artist) and then to the artist's tracks. Score both against the plain track-level model with the held-out log loss that dsdk.prob already provides, holding out one festival day at a time.
why it's interesting: On Sam's Lost Lands 2018 data a plain track-level model is almost blind across days. The probe below shows it cannot score about 3 in 4 held-out transitions at all, and for the ones it can score it is barely better than guessing uniformly. That makes "DJs play artists, not exact tracks" a testable claim, and it would turn the next-track instrument from a toy into something that predicts.
smallest experiment: Add an artist column to the transition graph that Manager W builds, fit one next-track model on artists and one on tracks, and report held-out log loss per festival day for three models: tracks only, artists only, and track-then-artist backoff. Keep the model that wins on all three held-out days and show the three numbers side by side in the Lab.
reuses: dsdk.prob fit_next_track and held_out_log_loss (the track-level model and the scoring function), dsdk.graph (a graph with artist nodes), the Lost Lands store that Manager W loads.
probe: Re-run with the layout Manager W confirmed (transition columns: source track, target track, DJ credit, date index; artist = primary artists joined with " & "). For each day with at least 50 transitions, train on all other dated days and score that day with smoothing 0.1. The track-level model can score only a minority of held-out transitions (both tracks seen elsewhere): 1 of 56, 74 of 168, 140 of 619, 98 of 569 and 112 of 419 for 2018-04-12 and 2018-09-13 to 2018-09-16. The artist-level model scores far more: 25, 129, 355, 294 and 231. Loss per scored transition in nats (lower is better): track level 7.18, 6.96, 6.83, 6.75, 6.91 against about 6.9 to 7.2 for guessing uniformly over the training tracks, so it barely beats guessing; artist level 5.82, 6.33, 6.30, 6.12, 6.27 against about 6.3 to 6.5 for guessing uniformly over artists, a gain of 0.0 to 0.7 nats that is clearest on the first and last days. Only 5% of all transitions (101 of 1,919) stay with the same artist, so the gain is not just "the same DJ plays again". Caveat: the two models are scored on different subsets of transitions, so the next experiment must compare them on the same transitions with the back-off rule. Command: fit_next_track and held_out_log_loss from dsdk.prob over the decoded store, grouped by transition column 3.
score: surprise=4 cost=2 reuse=5
